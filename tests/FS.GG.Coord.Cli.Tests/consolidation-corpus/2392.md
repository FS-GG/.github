The delivery-route receipt binds to a SHA-256 of the **entire issue body**, but `Paths:` and `Class:` are
mutable declaration lines that the protocol's own commands rewrite in that same body. So a routine,
required protocol action silently invalidates the receipt and makes the item unschedulable until a host
re-affirms it by hand.

## Observed — twice on 2026-08-11, by two different actors, in one session

| # | actor | action | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | drive-board host | `gh issue edit` on `.github#2361` to change its `Class:` body line `hardening` -> `defect` | `batch --explain` began refusing the row: `awaiting an explicit current delivery-route decision (subjectRevision is stale)` |
| 2 | claimed worker `plover-0209` | `scripts/fsgg-coord set-paths` on `.github#2249` (narrow, then widen back) | `delivery-route show .github#2249 --json` -> `subjectRevision is stale`, exit 1 |

`Verification:`
- `src/FS.GG.Coord.Cli/Client.fs:402` — `deliveryRouteRevision` is `body |> UTF8.GetBytes |> SHA256.HashData`, over the whole body with no field exclusion.
- `src/FS.GG.Coord.Core/DeliveryRoute.fs:53` — `if receipt.SubjectRevision <> expectedRevision then yield "subjectRevision is stale"`.
- `src/FS.GG.Coord.Core/Schedulability.fs:146-148` — `DeliveryRoute.Stale` maps to `AwaitingDeliveryRouteDecision`, which is what removes the row from the schedulable set.
- Case 1: `scripts/fsgg-coord batch --repo .github --explain` at 2026-08-11T13:38Z printed the stale-subjectRevision refusal for `#2361`.
- Case 2: `FSGG_WORKER=plover-0209 scripts/fsgg-coord delivery-route show FS-GG/.github#2249 --json` -> `malformed response reading FS-GG/.github#2249: subjectRevision is stale.`

## Root cause

The receipt's freshness binding is **too broad for what it is protecting**. Its purpose is to ensure a
route decision still describes the work it was judged against — scope, contracts, blast radius. But it is
bound to bytes that include lines whose mutation is:

- **required by the protocol** — `pnext-item` §3 says "If scope must grow, use `widen` before touching
  it", and `widen`/`set-paths` record that declaration by rewriting the body's `Paths:` line;
- **required by triage** — `Class:` in the body is the documented authority for classification
  (`drive-board` §10 and `backlog-triage`: "the authority is the item's own `Class:` body line, so never
  hand-edit the column"), and changing it is exactly how a host raises an item's rank.

So the two operations the protocol most explicitly instructs actors to perform are the two that break the
receipt. A `Paths:` widen is also **precisely the moment the scope actually changed**, which is the one
case where re-reading the route would be legitimate — but the mechanism cannot distinguish "scope grew,
re-judge the route" from "one word of prose changed", and treats a `Class:` edit and a typo fix with the
same severity as a genuine scope change.

## Why it is worse than a stale flag

A stale receipt is a **fail-closed refusal**, not a warning. The row leaves the schedulable set entirely,
and `claim` itself refuses with exit 1. Recovery requires an actor holding host authority to author a
replacement receipt — a claimed worker correctly refuses to author its own route judgement
(`.github#2298` puts that decision outside the worker). So:

1. worker widens as instructed ->
2. receipt goes stale ->
3. item becomes unschedulable and unclaimable ->
4. only the host can clear it, by re-recording a receipt whose judgement has not changed at all.

Observed cost in one session: three host re-affirmations (`#2361`, `#2249`, and `#2216` from a related
absence), each of them a pure revision refresh restating an identical route.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A `Paths:`-only or `Class:`-only edit to an item body does NOT invalidate an otherwise-current
   delivery-route receipt, OR the receipt records enough structure to re-affirm itself mechanically
   without a fresh human/host judgement.
2. A change that genuinely alters the route-relevant subject still invalidates the receipt. Whatever
   scheme is chosen must distinguish these two cases rather than widening the exemption until nothing
   invalidates it.
3. A widen that GROWS the touch-set is explicitly considered: if growth should re-open the route
   decision, that is a deliberate, documented choice with its own signal, not a side effect of hashing.
4. The chosen behaviour ships with gate-inversion evidence: a test that fails if a `Class:`-only edit
   invalidates the receipt, AND a test that fails if a genuinely route-relevant change does not.
5. Existing recorded receipts remain readable, or a stated migration path exists.
